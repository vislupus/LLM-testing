Understanding von Kármán Vortex Streets

A von Kármán vortex street is a repeating pattern of swirling vortices, caused by a process known as vortex shedding, which is responsible for the unsteady separation of flow of a fluid around blunt bodies.

Key Flow Regimes (Reynolds Number Re = u·D / ν)

  • Re < 5: Creeping Stokes flow. Completely attached, symmetric streamlines.
  • 5 < Re < 47: Steady laminar separation. Two symmetric, counter-rotating closed recirculating eddies attach behind the body.
  • 47 < Re < 180 (Sweet Spot): Classic laminar von Kármán vortex street! Shear layers become unstable and shed alternating vortices periodically.
  • Re > 180: Transition to three-dimensional instability and turbulent wake structures.

The Strouhal Number (St): The dimensionless shedding frequency is given by St = f · D / u∞. For circular cylinders in the subcritical regime, Strouhal number is famously constant around St ≈ 0.18 to 0.21!

How the Lattice-Boltzmann Method (LBM) Works: Rather than directly discretizing the macroscopic Navier-Stokes continuous equations, LBM simulates particle probability distributions fi on a 2D 9-velocity discrete lattice (D2Q9). Particles stream to neighboring grid points along 9 directions and collide locally towards a Maxwellian equilibrium distribution, naturally obeying conservation of mass and momentum!

Vortex-Induced Vibrations (VIV): As each vortex forms and detaches, it exerts an alternating transverse suction force on the body. This oscillating lift force causes singing telephone wires, underwater oil riser vibrations, and the historic collapse of the original Tacoma Narrows Bridge.
