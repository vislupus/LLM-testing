Key Facts

What Do We Know?

🌕

Earth's Only Moon

The Moon is Earth's only natural satellite. It appears nearly the same size as the Sun in our sky, but only because the Moon is much closer to us.

💡

Reflected Light

The Moon does not produce its own light. It simply reflects sunlight back to us — like a giant mirror in space!

🔭

Galileo's Discovery

Italian astronomer Galileo Galilei first observed the Moon with a telescope in 1608. He discovered its bumpy, uneven surface.

🌑

Craters & Dust

The Moon's surface is covered with a layer of dust and many craters — bowl-shaped holes formed by space rocks hitting it over millions of years.

🔄

Two Movements

The Moon makes two movements: it orbits Earth and it spins on its own axis. Since both take the same time, we always see the same side.

📅

Lunar Month

It takes the Moon 29 days and 12 hours to go through all its phases and return to the same shape. We call this a lunar month.

Review

What Did You Learn?

🌍

Earth's Companion

The Moon is Earth's only natural satellite. It orbits our planet while also spinning on its own axis.

🌓

Eight Phases

As the Moon orbits Earth, we see different portions of its lit face, creating the lunar phases over a 29.5-day cycle.

🌑

Lunar Eclipse

When Earth passes between the Sun and Moon, Earth's shadow covers the Moon — this is a lunar eclipse.

☀️

Solar Eclipse

When the Moon passes between the Sun and Earth, the Moon's shadow falls on Earth — this is a solar eclipse.

🔭

Galileo's Telescope

Galileo Galilei first observed the Moon's craters and uneven surface with a telescope in 1608.

🌐

Earth is Round!

Pythagoras observed that Earth's shadow on the Moon is always round — proving Earth is a sphere!
