Earth & The Moon

Discover our planet's only natural satellite — learn about its phases, movements, and the amazing eclipses it creates!

📖

What Do We Know?

🌍 The Moon — Earth's Companion

The Moon is Earth's only natural satellite. When we look at it in the night sky, it appears to be about the same size as the Sun. That's because, while the Sun is much bigger, it is also much farther away.

The Moon shines less brightly than the Sun because it does not produce its own light — it only reflects sunlight. The Moon's diameter is about 4 times smaller than Earth's and about 100 times smaller than the Sun's.

🔭 The Moon's Surface

The Moon has no water, no air, and no atmosphere. It was first observed through a telescope in 1608 by the Italian astronomer Galileo Galilei. He discovered that the Moon has an uneven surface covered with dust and many craters (bowl-shaped dents) that have been there for millions of years.

🔄 How the Moon Moves

The Moon makes two kinds of movement: it orbits around Earth and it spins on its own axis. The time it takes to spin once on its axis equals the time for one orbit around Earth. That's why we always see the same side of the Moon!

As the Moon orbits Earth, different amounts of its sunlit side are visible to us. We call these changing shapes Moon phases. The main phases are: New Moon → First Quarter → Full Moon → Last Quarter. The cycle between two identical phases takes 29 days and 12 hours — a lunar month.

🌑 Eclipses

Lunar eclipse: When Earth is between the Sun and the Moon, Earth's shadow falls on the Moon and blocks sunlight from reaching it. The ancient Greek mathematician Pythagoras (around 500 BC) concluded that Earth must be a sphere because its shadow on the Moon is round.

Solar eclipse: When the Moon is between the Sun and Earth, its shadow falls on Earth's surface and blocks the Sun. Because the Sun and the Moon appear almost the same size in the sky, the Moon can completely cover the Sun — and we see only the Sun's glowing corona. Solar eclipses are rare events at any given location.

Remember These Key Points

✦ Moon phases change because we see different amounts of the Moon's sunlit side as it orbits Earth.

✦ A lunar eclipse happens when the Moon enters Earth's shadow (Earth is between the Sun and Moon).

✦ A solar eclipse happens when the Moon's shadow falls on Earth (Moon is between the Sun and Earth).

✦ The Moon has no atmosphere, no water, and a surface covered with craters.

✦ We always see the same face of the Moon because it rotates once in exactly the same time it orbits Earth.

⚠️ Safety Warning!

Never look directly at the Sun without special protective glasses — it can permanently damage your eyes!
